# Timeline and decision gates
Updated September 25, 2026. Relative stages, not promised launch dates.

## Now — prepare the first batch
Align the mobile website with free friend finding and open Berkeley beta access. Offer a zero-spend preference. Keep existing onboarding and private invitation flow.
Before posting: verify production application storage, admin access, invitation/RSVP flow, required versus optional fields, and mobile layout. Send a real test through the production inbox with consent and remove test records afterward.
Prepare one concrete public plan and invite a few people personally. No automated email delivery is promised; founder sends invitation links.
Do not spend on a venue or inventory before attendance is understood.

## Next — three small gatherings
Aim for six; four confirmed people is a valid pilot. Use available public spaces. Founder cap $0–$30 per gathering, with any personal purchases optional.
Confirm exact availability and reconfirm the day before. Record actual attendance and private feedback. Help willing members make a second plan.
Optional phone-down experiment: voluntary, phones accessible, no lockout or surveillance. Publish testimonials only with permission.
Gate: evidence that people show up and some meet again. If not, investigate timing, location, group fit, and format before adding features.

## Alongside the pilot — buyer discovery
Interview five relevant community or brand budget owners about their last purchased campaign, objectives, actual spending, and buying process.
Distinguish sponsorship from sales-lead generation. Request a concrete scoped paid pilot; compliments are not demand.
No assumption that six student members are an enterprise buyer audience.

## After local repeat attendance — optional SF dinners
Offer an occasional San Francisco dinner only when enough existing members explicitly want to travel and accept the budget. The LARP tier is playful, optional, and never the default school-year plan. Confirm interest before reservations or spending. Berkeley remains the regular local community.

## After evidence — one sponsored experience
Only proceed with a committed payer and a budget covering obligations.
Clearly disclose the sponsor in invitations. Invite members who opted into relevant interests; any business contact-sharing requires separate consent.
Founder can assemble a small number of thoughtful kits. Gifts and premium decor are optional line items, not launch requirements.
Track costs, labor, sponsor objective, attendee response, and whether either side wants another event.

## After reorders — repeatable operations
Standardize formats, suppliers, host training, purchasing limits, and event records. Test paid Life of the Party hosting assignments.
Delegate one event without founder intervention; measure quality and operator time.
Add basic ticketing and host phone QR scanning only when paid events need it. Consider native Tap to Pay later.

## Later — community network and expansion
Introduce references as demand grows, preserving a path for newcomers. Trial recurring sponsor programs and optional premium planning.
Add event/place pins first. Any people-location sharing must be explicit, temporary, audience-limited, and stoppable; precise GPS is never required for matching.
Expand locations or build native apps when repeat demand and operational evidence justify them.
